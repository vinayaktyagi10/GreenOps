The problem

The same job, run at the wrong hour, can emit multiples more CO₂.

Electricity grids swing between clean and dirty all day, driven by the live mix of wind, solar, gas, and coal on the line. Almost nothing that schedules compute today — cron, Kubernetes, batch queues — treats that as a signal. A report job kicked off at 2pm on a coal-heavy grid can cost several times the carbon of the identical job run at 2am on a wind-heavy one, or shifted to a cleaner region entirely. GreenOps makes that signal a first-class citizen in scheduling, right alongside CPU and memory.

How it works

One workflow, from submission to a running (or waiting) container.

01
Submit a workload

Name, estimated runtime, and namespace — same as any job queue.

02
Choose a priority tier

Critical / Standard / Flexible — how much the job is allowed to wait for a cleaner grid.

critical never waits
03
Fetch live + forecasted carbon intensity

Electricity Maps data across simulated regions, refreshed continuously.

04
Score with the policy engine

Weighs current intensity, forecast trend, urgency, and namespace carbon budget into a single decision score.

05
Execute now, or hold

Clean enough → a real Docker container is created immediately. Not yet → the job waits and is re-scored on the next tick.

06
Log and report

Every decision is recorded — carbon at execution, delay time, region — feeding the dashboard and the LLM summary.
